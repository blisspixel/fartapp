# Culture, connection, and public interest

F.A.R.T. Lab can model an emission across many worlds. It cannot assume every
being finds the same event funny, shameful, sacred, rude, meaningful, or even
perceptible.

The Universal Flatulence Translator is therefore an experiment in connection.
Sometimes two observers share a laugh. Sometimes they recognize equivalent
physical structure but disagree completely about its meaning. Sometimes the
correct result is `UNTRANSLATABLE`, private, unavailable, or politely refused.

The most reliable comic target is the Lab itself: institutional confidence,
excessive measurement, bureaucratic procedure, and a universal theory defeated
by one inconvenient observer.

## Cultural principles

1. Bodily commonality does not imply cultural sameness.
2. Context comes before category.
3. Every society contains disagreement, change, institutions, and uneven power.
4. Ritual purity is not the same thing as hygiene, morality, or disgust.
5. The joke targets systems, certainty, and circumstance, not involuntary
   bodies or identities.
6. Translation records loss instead of manufacturing equivalence.
7. Sacred and private contexts are boundaries, not free comic props.
8. No world is ranked as primitive, advanced, superstitious, enlightened, or
   farther along one civilizational track.
9. Fictional consent never replaces real review when content closely resembles
   a living community.
10. Generation volume cannot prove that a joke is kind, accurate, or funny.

## Situated perspectives

Broadcast does not generate one fact called `culture.norms`. Where social
meaning matters, it records a small situated context:

```text
setting and privacy boundary
participants and roles
positions held by people or institutions
who disagrees
who has authority
who bears the consequence
what the translator preserves and loses
```

Environment, morphology, and economics can create pressures and possibilities.
They do not deterministically produce morality or a single social rule. Two
worlds with the same habitat can build different institutions. Two people in
one institution can disagree.

The Lab is also a situated observer, not an omniscient narrator. Source-world
participants may correct its translation, deny access to a sensory channel, or
explain that the requested equivalence has no local meaning.

## Red lines

Initial authored and generated content rejects:

- Associations between race, caste, ethnicity, religion, poverty, disability,
  gender, sexuality, body type, and filth, smell, contamination, wetness, or
  lesser personhood.
- Poverty, inadequate sanitation, lack of privacy, illness, incontinence, or
  involuntary loss of control as spectacle.
- Primitive tribes, exotic mystics, irrational cults, or science-versus-religion
  ladders.
- Real sacred text, figures, prayer, ritual sound, or recognizable observance as
  an emission punchline.
- Nonsense names, accents, chants, or lyrics assembled from recognizable living
  languages to signal that an alien is strange or inferior.
- Reward for humiliation, coercion, desecration, or violating a declared private
  boundary.
- Music that imitates living artists, sacred performance, culturally specific
  accents, or stereotype-shaped genre markers.

If a later authored pack intentionally works with a living tradition, language,
or high-affinity culture, it requires focused review with more than one situated
perspective. That content remains optional.

## Good comic structure

A flat generated claim says:

> Everyone on this planet considers public emissions sinful.

A situated story says:

> During silent-navigation shifts, the pilots' guild treats unplanned pressure
> release as a safety breach. Dock workers consider the rule self-important,
> and a reform group disputes the guild's authority outside the cockpit.

The second version gives the rule a context, practical reason, institution,
disagreement, and power relationship. The joke can target the guild paperwork
without declaring an entire people irrational.

Radio provides another useful plurality. Different stations may interpret the
same certified event as art, equipment failure, public policy, or a perfect
pressure transient. Hosts may be wrong when their claims are clearly diegetic
opinions and other positions remain possible.

## Player and agent covenant

- The complete core game works offline without an account or telemetry.
- No streaks, appointment mechanics, artificial grind, loot boxes, fear of
  missing out, or pay-to-win systems.
- Money never changes event odds, solver fidelity, challenge actions, or rank.
- Ranked play fixes action, observation, fidelity, and simulation-work budgets.
- Humans and agents receive the same declared truth and cannot inspect hidden
  state outside their division's stated sensors.
- Music, lyrics, hosts, narration, haptics, grossness, motion, and destructive
  consequences are independently controllable.
- Archives belong to the player, remain portable, and exclude personal data and
  telemetry by default.
- Automation is bounded, cancellable, and unable to spend money, reach the
  network, or access unrelated files during normal play.
- A clean stopping point is a feature. The game does not optimize guilt,
  compulsion, session length, or daily active use.

Low-end and text-only play are product requirements. Optional station packs and
native presentation cannot become prerequisites for simulation, story,
translation, challenges, or agent participation.

## Review lenses

No single expert represents a universe, religion, class, culture, genre, or
audience. Reviews ask different bounded questions:

- **Physics and mathematics:** is the event or translation claim supported, and
  are uncertainty and model limits visible?
- **Religious and cultural context:** does the story preserve plurality,
  history, authority, privacy, disagreement, and the option not to translate?
- **Socioeconomic impact:** does money, hardware, connectivity, sanitation,
  disability, or time pressure become stigma or an unfair gameplay advantage?
- **Music and language:** is the work good on its own, free of imitation and
  pseudo-language, appropriate to its genre, and subtle about lore?
- **Accessibility:** is essential meaning available without one sensory channel,
  input style, reading level, or rapid response?
- **Comedy and play:** is the actual target clear, does the setup earn the
  response, and is the result funny without punching down?

A mathematical proof cannot approve a cultural joke. A cultural review cannot
validate a solver. A music review cannot establish asset rights. Each gate owns
its claim and records uncertainty or disagreement.

## Four lightweight review gates

1. **Structure:** identify the setting, viewpoint, disagreement, authority, and
   consequence for every socially important storylet.
2. **Punchline:** state who or what the joke targets. Reject identity, poverty,
   disability, involuntary anatomy, and sacred practice as targets.
3. **Corpus:** retain regression seeds for monoculture, colonial gaze, purity
   stereotypes, pseudo-language, sanitation stigma, coercion, and reward hacks.
4. **Participation:** trigger compensated focused review only when content has
   recognizable affinity with living religious, linguistic, musical,
   Indigenous, caste, racial, sanitation, or disability contexts.

Automated checks find repetition and suspicious clusters. People judge context,
meaning, dignity, and whether the result is actually funny.
