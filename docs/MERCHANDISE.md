# Merchandise principles

Merchandise is a possible extension of the open project, not a dependency, a
funding promise, or a reason to weaken the scientific joke.

The strongest system has three layers:

1. **Core Marks:** stable official name, Open Isobar, Pressure Standard identity,
   and release marks under a reviewed trademark policy.
2. **Community Kit:** clearly unofficial, openly remixable frames, glyphs,
   captions, and scientific-comedy primitives.
3. **Event Artifacts:** private or shareable one-time Plumeprints, Fartflakes,
   observation cards, posters, shirts, patches, and sculptures.

Apache License 2.0 applies to repository material only as declared. It does not
silently make project trademarks, music, fonts, generated art, or third-party
media unrestricted. The intended Community Kit license is CC0 after ownership
and policy review. Core Marks remain downloadable at production quality but use
is governed separately so origin and verification stay meaningful.

## First pilot

Begin only after name, mark, entity, tax, privacy, supplier, product, consumer,
and insurance review. Low-complexity pilot candidates are:

- Adult shirt.
- Embroidered patch.
- Vinyl sticker.
- Archival laboratory notebook.
- Numbered observation print.

There are no randomized drops, loot mechanics, paid scientific status,
engagement-gated releases, or manufactured scarcity. Numbering identifies a
production batch, not an irreproducible entitlement. Reprint policy is public,
local print-ready export remains available, and apparel does not launch without
an inclusive size range and actual garment measurements.

Delay toys, children's products, electronics, cosmetics, consumables, scented
products, and food-contact goods until their category-specific requirements are
understood.

Each supplier must pass two physical samples, wash and dry cycles where
applicable, shrinkage, cracking, color transfer, embroidery pull, placement,
color, seam, and blank inspection. A supplier or process change triggers new
sampling. Approved SKUs record artwork version, supplier, region, batch, support,
defect, refund, recall, and unauthorized-overrun controls.

Environmental, labor, material, and certification claims name the evidence and
SKU scope. The project does not use unqualified “eco-friendly,” “zero impact,”
or “sustainable” language.

## Store boundary

Use a separate hosted storefront. The CLI and native app contain at most a plain
official-store reference. They do not contain advertising, purchase telemetry,
checkout state, payment code, or a merch account.

The store collects only information needed for fulfillment, payment, fraud,
tax, and support, with documented retention and deletion. Event details remain
private unless the buyer deliberately submits a print artifact. Accessibility
targets WCAG 2.2 AA, keyboard-complete checkout, meaningful product alt text,
text size charts, actual garment measurements, sensory details, and no
color-only distinction.

## Event artifacts

Local print-ready export comes before official fulfillment. A buyer may choose
to submit one artifact without uploading the source archive, hidden world state,
precise location, or personal machine data. The item says `SIMULATED EVENT` and
`OBSERVED ONCE`. It carries a trace identifier, not a replay promise.

An official SKU code authenticates the item and batch only. It does not identify
the buyer or claim scientific, safety, or environmental certification.

## Community goods

The planned permissive policy should allow commercial use of only the Community
Kit when the seller:

- Makes its own identity more prominent.
- Labels the listing and packaging `UNOFFICIAL COMMUNITY GOODS`.
- Does not use a Core Mark, Pressure Standard seal, or official SKU device.
- Does not imply endorsement, verification, safety, or public authority.
- Complies with applicable product, advertising, privacy, and consumer law.

Official Core Mark merchandise for sale requires written permission. The open
community path should be simple enough that creators do not need to ask.

## Revenue and contributor expectations

Before the first sale, publish that a purchase is not a donation and that an
ordinary source contribution does not create a merchandise royalty. Paid design
or revenue-sharing work requires a separate written agreement.

If revenue becomes meaningful, report gross sales, discounts, refunds,
manufacturing, fulfillment, platform and payment fees, tax, artist payments,
net revenue, and project or community allocation. Do not claim that “profits
support the project” without defining profit.

Official logo work requires a written agreement covering authority to use,
modify, register, license, and enforce the design; source files; originality;
third-party disclosure; permitted omission of a public byline; compensation;
and appropriate moral-rights terms. Do not choose the official mark through an
informal public contest.

## Authenticity and enforcement

A public registry should list authorized store domains, vendors, SKUs, artwork
versions, regions, batches, and support. Response focuses on confusion, safety,
and counterfeit representation:

1. Confirm the actual problem.
2. Request friendly clarification where appropriate.
3. Offer migration to the open Community Kit.
4. Use marketplace procedures for persistent deceptive commercial use.
5. Escalate safety incidents and intentional counterfeits promptly.
6. Preserve criticism, parody, nominative reference, and ordinary jokes.

The project enforces against confusion, not against humor.

This is a product and research plan, not legal advice. Relevant professional
review gates are listed in the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md).
