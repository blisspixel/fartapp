# Simulated computer-science design review

This is a deliberately simulated design discussion with an enthusiastic,
demanding computer-science PhD persona. It is not presented as an interview with
a real person or as external validation. Its purpose is to pressure-test the
project across scientific computing, programming languages, HCI, learning
science, procedural narrative, reproducibility, and game design.

## Discussion

**Reviewer:** The concept is stronger than the joke. The fart is not decoration
on a simulation platform. It is a shared coordinate system for thermodynamics,
numerical methods, reproducibility, procedural narrative, HCI, and scientific
skepticism.

**Project:** What should the player actually learn?

**Reviewer:** Not a list of equations. Give the player an epistemic loop:
predict, simulate, observe, explain, vary one cause, and try to falsify the
explanation. A three-second pfft can teach more than a page of exposition when
the counterfactual is immediate and runnable.

**Project:** We require one authoritative event history. Is that the right
abstraction?

**Reviewer:** Preserve one physical truth, but do not force every consumer into
one master-rate array. Fields, observer samples, audio, narration, and rendering
operate at different rates and resolutions. Use an immutable provenance graph.
Every projection should cite its inputs, transformation, clock, uncertainty,
and hash.

**Project:** How universal can the Universal Flatulence Translator be?

**Reviewer:** Universal as a protocol, not as one equation. A law profile
declares capabilities and invariants. Translation requires an explicit
compatibility witness. Some mappings are strict, some approximate, some
perceptual, and some impossible. `UNTRANSLATABLE` is one of the lab's best
results.

**Project:** Can tooling generate the meaningful dimensionless groups?

**Reviewer:** It can prove that a group is dimensionless and compute possible
bases. It generally cannot infer the uniquely meaningful scientific basis from
equations alone. Let law-pack authors declare stable semantic groups. Have the
tooling verify them, trace their dependencies, and identify possible omissions.

**Project:** Is the Go oracle plus Rust core a sound plan?

**Reviewer:** Only if the Go oracle stays tiny and independently formulated. Two
implementations of the same mistaken derivation do not provide independent
verification. Freeze the oracle's scope, pair it with exact and manufactured
cases, and do not accidentally build two products.

**Project:** How do we connect cutting-edge theory without becoming
pretentious?

**Reviewer:** Require every advanced idea to change a player action, an
observable, or a test. Type theory should prevent invalid states. Dimensional
analysis should enable scaling challenges. Provenance should make explanations
auditable. Counter-based randomness should make stories replayable. If a theory
produces only impressive vocabulary, leave it out.

**Project:** How do we prove Broadcast is fun?

**Reviewer:** Ten thousand terminating seeds prove generator robustness, not
comedy. Maintain a curated seed museum, a failure corpus, repetition metrics,
contradiction checks, and regular human playtests. Humor still needs editorial
judgment.

**Project:** What should ship first?

**Reviewer:** One excellent ordinary pfft, end to end. It should start instantly,
sound funny, expose its seed, explain one surprising fact, produce a small
evidence bundle, and invite one meaningful variation. Build that walking
skeleton before the archive and architecture become elaborate.

## Commitments accepted into the plan

1. Move the first playable and inspectable ordinary pfft into the Go-oracle
   milestone, then preserve it through the Rust migration.
2. Model one physical truth as a typed, immutable, multirate provenance graph.
3. Give law profiles explicit capabilities, compatibility witnesses, trust
   classes, and unsupported states.
4. Freeze the Go oracle's scope and compare both implementations with
   independently derived references.
5. Make predict, emit, inspect, explain, vary, and transfer a first-class play
   loop.
6. Separate scenario, physical-result, narrative, presentation, and archive-byte
   identities.
7. Treat semantic Pi groups as authored scientific contracts that tooling
   verifies, not meanings automatically discovered from a matrix.
8. Report sensitivity, nonuniqueness, model-form uncertainty, extrapolation, and
   inter-fidelity disagreement.
9. Evaluate entertainment with a seed museum, failure corpus, editorial review,
   and representative human playtests.
10. Publish measurable CLI startup, first-output, run, cancellation, memory, and
    archive-size budgets on named reference systems.

## Questions the implementation must answer

- Can a player predict vacuum acoustics, choking, conservation, or similarity
  more accurately after play and transfer the idea to a new scenario?
- Can every audio block, rendered frame, damage event, and lab-fact sentence
  traverse provenance back to authoritative event state?
- Can strict translation produce a compatibility witness and round-trip within
  its declared tolerance?
- Can approximate translation expose residuals, sensitivity, nonuniqueness, and
  infeasibility without burying the answer?
- Can untrusted content remain expressive while staying declarative, offline,
  bounded, and incapable of loading code?
- Do ordinary low-energy seeds remain funny, replayable, and shareable without
  relying on spectacle?
- Can a new contributor understand why a result is trusted from the CLI alone?

The design target is not advanced theory wearing a fart costume. It is a
playable demonstration of how knowledge is built: declare assumptions, make a
prediction, preserve evidence, admit uncertainty, test a counterfactual, and
laugh when reality produces a polite ripple instead of the promised apocalypse.
